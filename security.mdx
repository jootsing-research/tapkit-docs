## What's on the iPhone

Nothing is installed on the iPhone. TapKit uses two things built into iOS:

- **Screen capture over USB**, which requires the iPhone to trust the Mac.
- **A Bluetooth mouse and keyboard**, which the Mac pretends to be, with AssistiveTouch turning mouse input into touches.

TapKit can't bypass the passcode, Face ID, or anything else a person using the phone couldn't. To cut TapKit off, unplug the phone, quit the Mac app, or forget the Mac in **Settings > Bluetooth**.

## Mac permissions

| Permission | Used for | Not used for |
|---|---|---|
| Camera | Receiving the iPhone's screen over USB | Your Mac's webcam |
| Bluetooth | Pairing with the iPhone and sending touch and keyboard input | Any other Bluetooth device |

## Where screenshots and actions go

- **Built-in agent.** Screenshots are captured on your Mac and sent to a hosted language model through TapKit's servers to decide the next action. Actions go straight from your Mac to the phone.
- **MCP and REST API.** Commands travel from your agent through TapKit's servers to your Mac. Screenshots travel back the same way and are stored by TapKit. Your agent's provider (for example Anthropic or OpenAI) receives the screenshots your agent requests.
- **Live streaming.** When you view a phone in the web app, its screen is streamed through Agora.

The Mac app shows each phone's screen locally, but only sends it off the Mac when an agent takes a screenshot or someone opens a live stream.

## Access control

- **Mac app** — signs in through tapkit.ai and keeps its session in the macOS Keychain.
- **MCP clients** — sign in with OAuth and see a consent screen before connecting. Access tokens expire after an hour and are refreshed automatically.
- **REST API** — uses API keys from the [web app](/web-app#api-keys). TapKit stores only a hash of each key. Revoked keys stop working immediately.

Anyone in your organization can use its phones.

## Agent safety

TapKit's agent skills tell agents to verify typed text and stop before submitting it unless you've asked them to, and not to type passwords you haven't provided. These reduce risk but don't remove it: an agent with your phone can do anything you can. Use a phone and accounts you're comfortable letting an agent use.
